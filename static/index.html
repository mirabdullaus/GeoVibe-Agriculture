NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Project prototype

Understanding agricultural fields through Earth observation.

GeoVibe Agriculture is an interactive platform that brings satellite-based vegetation indicators, climate context, historical trends and spatial change detection into one field-scale geospatial interface.

Users can select an agricultural field, explore satellite observations, compare historical patterns and investigate environmental changes without writing geospatial code.

Why GeoVibe Agriculture?

Vegetation monitoring

Explore NDVI, NDMI and EVI derived from Sentinel-2 and Landsat imagery within a selected field.

Climate context

Compare gridded precipitation records with vegetation observations while recognising their different spatial resolutions.

Change investigation

Examine historical observations, estimate trends and visualize spatial differences between periods.

Understanding your results

Each value describes the selected polygon and study period. Calculations use available satellite observations after cloud and quality filtering; masked pixels are excluded. A high valid-area percentage describes unmasked coverage, not a guarantee of a cloud-free image.

NDVI · Greenness

NDVI = (NIR − Red) / (NIR + Red). NIR means near-infrared reflectance. Higher NDVI generally indicates stronger green vegetation signal, but it is not a direct crop-health measurement.

NDMI · Canopy moisture response

NDMI = (NIR − SWIR1) / (NIR + SWIR1). SWIR1 is the shortwave-infrared band. NDMI is sensitive to vegetation water content and background conditions; it is not measured soil moisture.

EVI · Enhanced vegetation index

EVI = 2.5 × (NIR − Red) / (NIR + 6 × Red − 7.5 × Blue + 1). EVI modifies the vegetation response to reduce some background and atmospheric influences.

NDVI and NDMI differences

Each difference below the metric is current value − historical reference average for comparable periods. For example, NDVI 0.44 with a difference of −0.22 means the comparison baseline was approximately 0.66. Index differences are not percentages.

Precipitation and percentage change

Precipitation is expressed in millimetres (mm) using NASA POWER gridded climate data. Percentage change = 100 × (current − historical average) / historical average. For example, −57% means 57% less precipitation than the comparison average, not zero rainfall.

Crop Condition Score: three categories

This experimental historical index is shown from 0 to 100: Good (65–100), Moderate (35–64), and Poor (0–34). It describes relative vegetation conditions compared with earlier matching periods; it is not a direct or validated crop-health diagnosis. These category boundaries are provisional interface conventions.

How historical comparisons and trends are produced

The analysis compares matching calendar months or seasons from previous years when data are available. The Crop Condition Score comes from the backend's experimental historical comparison; its precise scoring formula must be verified in app.py before describing it as a scientifically validated metric. Trends display a slope per year, a p-value and a 95% interval where enough comparable periods exist. A p-value of 0.136, for example, does not provide evidence of a statistically significant trend at the 0.05 level. Satellite observations and precipitation data have different spatial resolutions and should not be treated as identical measurements.

Scientific scope and limitations

Cloud and image quality

Cloud masking reduces contamination but cannot guarantee that every haze, shadow or mixed pixel has been removed.

Crop Condition Score

The score is an experimental 0–100 comparison of vegetation indices with matching historical periods. It is not a percentage of healthy crops or a yield estimate. The interface uses three descriptive categories: Good (65–100), Moderate (35–64), and Poor (0–34). Thresholds are provisional, not scientifically validated crop-health boundaries.

Cross-sensor comparisons

Sentinel-2 and Landsat have different sensor characteristics and resolutions. Interpretation must consider these differences.
